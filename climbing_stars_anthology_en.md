# The Vertical World: People Who Moved Climbing, Mountaineering, Bouldering and Related Disciplines (1900–2026)

> English translation of the Russian anthology `climbing_stars_anthology.md`. A living document: corrections and expansions continue as the source is revised. 
> Archival copy of an earlier version: `climbing_stars_report_claude.ORIGINAL.md`.

## On method

This is first of all an **anthology of people** — those who set the bar, the style, the technique, the ethics, or the public image of the vertical disciplines. Chronicles of styles and reference lists of achievements are subordinate to the portraits.

The report draws on open sources in many languages: Wikipedia (priority: Russian → English → any available), American Alpine Club / AAJ, the Piolets d'Or archive, Planetmountain, UKClimbing, Climbing.com, Alpinist, ExplorersWeb, Himalayan Database, mountainguide.ru, mountain.ru, specialist biographies and obituaries. For each person the main confirmed achievement is given with a date, birth year / lifespan and — where relevant — circumstance of death and, where possible, altitude, face, route and grade.

**Geography.** The Japanese, Sino-Tibetan, Sherpa and Pakistani strands, South America (Patagonia/Andes), Africa and the wingsuit contour are deliberately expanded — not as an “appendix to Europe,” but as independent lines of history.

The **commercial pole** (speed 14×8000, record Sherpa ascents, media operators) remains **inside the canon**: without it one cannot see how much harder it becomes, decade by decade, to stay niche.

**Discipline.** For key names, after country/dates — a short tag of the main contour: *mountaineering (high-altitude)*, *rock climbing (sport/trad)*, *bouldering*, *comps*, *competition alpinism*, *big walls*, *wingsuit/BASE*, etc. An orientation, not a rigid taxonomy: many lived at the seams.

### Achievement status badges

| Badge | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[confirmed]** | Achievement recognized by the community / documented enough for consensus |
| **[disputed]** | Serious arguments exist for and against; no single verdict |
| **[not accepted by the community]** | Most historians/the community consider the claim unreliable or ethically rejected as an “ascent” |

## Grades: how to read the numbers and letters in the text

**Sport climbing, French scale** (7c+, 8c, 9a, 9b, 9c) — an open scale with no upper limit; growth is nonlinear: 9a is orders of magnitude harder than 7a. Rough guide: 6a–6c — solid recreational level; 7a–7c — strong club climber; 8a and above — professional sport; 9a and above — a few dozen people worldwide.

**Sport climbing, American scale (YDS)** (5.9, 5.12a, 5.14b) — roughly maps to French (5.12a ≈ 7a+, 5.14b ≈ 8c+).

**UIAA** (Roman numerals I–XI+) — the historic German–Austrian scale. Roughly: VI ≈ French 6a, X ≈ 8a.

**Bouldering, V / Fontainebleau scale** (V4, 9A/8C+) — 9A/V17 is today’s generally recognized world ceiling. **Do not confuse** with the identically named French sport scale: Font 7A+ ≈ V8, whereas sport 7a+ ≈ 5.12a.

**Mountaineering, French overall seriousness scale** (F → PD → AD → D → TD → ED) — length, objective hazards, required speed — not the difficulty of a single move.

**Mountaineering, Soviet/Russian difficulty category** (1Б–6Б) — 1Б — simple movement on a rope team; 4А–5Б — multi-day routes with pitches of V and harder; 6А–6Б — the highest category, formally without a ceiling.

## Before 1900: foundations

**[Edward Whymper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Whymper)** (United Kingdom, 1840–1911) — **14 July 1865:** first ascent of the Matterhorn (4478 m) — the last of the great unclimbed Alpine peaks. Triumph turned to tragedy: four of the seven on the rope died on the descent when the rope broke. Died in 1911 in Chamonix — at the heart of the mountaineering world.

**[Georg Winkler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georg_Winkler)** (Germany, Munich) — founder of solo climbing in the modern sense.
- **1887:** at 17, alone, climbed the south tower of the Vajolet Towers (Dolomites, ~2820 m), considered impassable, in canvas shoes instead of nailed boots.
- Died in **1888** in an avalanche on the Weisshorn (4506 m), aged 18.

**[Angelo Dibona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelo_Dibona)** (Italy, Cortina, 1879–1956) — virtuoso guide of the Dolomites and Eastern Alps; dozens of first ascents before the era of mass “piton engineering.” The model guide for whom a route is a craft, not propaganda.

**[Tita Piaz](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tita_Piaz)** (“Devil of the Dolomites,” Italy, 1879–1950) — early free and solo climbing; a vivid political and ethical antipode to twentieth-century state mountaineering.

## 1900–1905: quiet transition

A period without individual “stars” tightly tied to the five-year slice — a transitional time when guideless climbing spread through the Eastern Alps and Dolomites in Winkler’s wake. In the same decade modern *sangaku* (mountain) tourism is formalized in Japan; in the Andes and the Caucasus local guides remain almost nameless in the European press — a gap that later chronicles are only beginning to close.

## 1906–1910: the Saxon phenomenon

**[Oliver Perry-Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Perry-Smith)** (USA → Germany, 1884–1969) — an American from a prominent Philadelphia family who moved to Dresden in 1902 and stayed twelve years; local rules forbade metal protection for progress (only for belay backup).
- **1906:** first ascent of “Perrykante” on Spannagelturm, grade VIIb.
- More than 90 ascents and 32 first ascents in Saxon Switzerland. Died in 1969, aged 84.

## 1911–1915: “Mauerhakenstreit” — the piton war

**[Paul Preuss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Preuss_(climber))** (Austria) — ideologue of “clean style”: the piton is cheating the mountain, not sport. His opponent in the public debate over pitons (“Mauerhakenstreit”) was **[Hans Dülfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_D%C3%BClfer)**, inventor of the Dülfersitz rappel and pendulum technique on pitons; killed on the Western Front of World War I in 1915, aged 23.
- **1911:** Preuss — unroped solo of the east face of Campanile Basso (Brenta, 2880 m), including the descent by the same line without a rope.
- Preuss died **3 October 1913**, aged 27, falling on the north ridge of Mandlkogel (2342 m) attempting a solo first ascent.

**[Otto Herzog](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Herzog_(Bergsteiger))**, nicknamed “Rambo” (Germany, 1888–1964) — one of the strongest rock climbers of the Eastern Alps of his generation. Around 1910–1913, with Hans Fichtl, he actively used carabiners on hard routes and in pendulum / tension-traverse technique (*Seilquergang*). Carabiners already existed in mountaineering and rescue: Herzog’s contribution was bold *application* on limiting routes, not invention of the carabiner itself. **1921:** with Gustav Haber climbed the 300-metre dihedral “Ha-He” on Dreizinkenspitze (Austria, VI+ UIAA) — one of the first alpine routes of that standard.

**[Willo Welzenbach](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willo_Welzenbach)** (Germany, 1900–1934) — co-author of the modern UIAA scale; later — the north face of the Grandes Jorasses. Would die on Nanga Parbat in 1934 — a link between the “piton war” and the “mountain of destiny.”

## 1916–1920: the “White War” and a technological pause

World War I turned the Dolomites into a front line. From 1915 to 1918 Italian and Austro-Hungarian troops fought the “White War” (*Guerra Bianca*) at altitudes up to 3900 m, cutting trenches and barracks into the glaciers themselves (the famous “Ice City” on the Marmolada). Most of the roughly 150–180 thousand dead on this theatre were taken not by combat but by cold, frostbite and avalanches.

**13 December 1916** — a day remembered as “White Friday” (by the calendar it was a **Wednesday**): a series of avalanches across the Alpine front. The largest single episode — an avalanche on the Austro-Hungarian barracks at Gran Poz on the Marmolada (**about 270–332** dead). By modern estimates (University of Bern / ECMWF, 2016) total avalanche deaths on 12–13 December across the front were on the order of **up to ~2000**; some slides may have been triggered by artillery on snow slopes. Military necessity drove mass production of tricouni nails and steel carabiners, which after 1918 passed into civilian mountaineering.

## 1921–1925: birth of the Soviet system

**[Nikolai Krylenko](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Крыленко,_Николай_Васильевич)** — Soviet state official, one of the organizers of early Soviet mountaineering as a state, planned and militarized project — a model fundamentally different from Western clubs of enthusiasts. He held that role while already a prosecutor who organized the indictment at the major Stalinist show trials. In 1938 he himself was arrested and shot on 29 July — in the case of a “counter-revolutionary fascist-terrorist organization of mountaineers and tourists” (the investigation’s wording of the era). The man who institutionalized Soviet mountaineering was destroyed by the same repression machine he had helped build, and formally — through his mountaineering activity. Rehabilitated posthumously in 1955.

**[George Mallory](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9C%D1%8D%D0%BB%D0%BB%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B8,_%D0%94%D0%B6%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B4%D0%B6) and [Andrew Irvine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Irvine_(mountaineer))** (United Kingdom) — **8 June 1924:** last seen alive approaching the summit of Everest, then disappeared. Whether they reached the top is unknown **[disputed / unproven]**; that would have made them first ascentionists 29 years before Hillary and Tenzing. Mallory’s body was found in 1999; in September 2024 a boot with a foot was found on the Rongbuk Glacier, identified by the label “A. C. IRVINE” as Irvine’s remains — a century later.


## 1926–1930: Fontainebleau

**[Pierre Allain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Allain)** (France, 1904–2000) begins training on the boulders of the forest of Fontainebleau near Paris — the start of the French school of bouldering; the famous “Allain’s Angle” (V2–V3), climbed in 1934, set a new standard of difficulty for the discipline. In 1935 with Raymond Leininger he climbed the north face of the Petit Dru using a 7 mm hemp rope, six carabiners, no crampons and a single ice axe. In the 1930s he developed the first rubber rock shoes (“PA”), the model for all later generations. Died in 2000, aged 96.

## 1931–1935: Soviet triumph, a Japanese soloist and the Abalakov brothers

**[Yevgeny Abalakov](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Абалаков,_Евгений_Михайлович)** (USSR, 1907–1948) — sculptor and mountaineer; grew up on the **[Krasnoyarsk Stolby](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A1%D1%82%D0%BE%D0%BB%D0%B1%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D1%8B)** with his brother — the “stolbist” school gave both a rock foundation before the big mountains.
- **3 September 1933:** first person in the world to reach the main (south) dome of Peak Stalin (7495 m, now Ismoil Somoni Peak, the highest point of the USSR). He went to the main summit essentially alone: expedition leader Nikolai Gorbunov reached the north dome below the true top and turned back with altitude sickness; the other storm-party members also failed to arrive.
- In the war — defence of the Caucasus and teaching at the military mountaineering school.
- Died in Moscow on the night of **24 March 1948** together with Yuri Artsishevsky; the official version — carbon monoxide poisoning from a faulty heater. Even then the version raised doubts among some contemporaries.

There were in fact three Abalakov brothers — elder Mikhail (1904), **[Vitaly Abalakov](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Абалаков,_Виталий_Михайлович)** (1906–1986) and Yevgeny — but two entered mountaineering history: Mikhail did not practise the sport. Vitaly invented the ice-screw self-extraction loop (“Abalakov thread”), still used unchanged; multiple champion and Honoured Coach of the USSR — organizer of the Soviet training system.

**[Riccardo Cassin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riccardo_Cassin)** (Italy, 1909–2009) — self-taught former blacksmith’s apprentice, founder of the “Nuovi Mai” mountain group in Lecco, begins a series of first ascents that will lead him to the Walker Spur five years later. Later he will open the north-east face of Piz Badile (1937) and the Cassin Ridge on Denali (1961); his firm for decades produced carabiners and ice tools. Lived to 100, still climbing in deep old age.

**[Naomi Uemura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Uemura)** (Japan, 1941–1984) — symbol of Japanese solo exploration: **24 April 1965** — first ascent of Ngojumba Kang II with Pemba Tenzing Sherpa; first Japanese solo on Everest (**1970**), winter solo of Denali, solo North Pole. The Japanese line of the soloist and the national expedition machine develops in parallel with the European. Will disappear without trace on Denali in winter 1984.

**[Willo Welzenbach](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willo_Welzenbach)** and members of the German Nanga Parbat expedition — **1934:** the disaster that took Welzenbach and many others; the “mountain of destiny” enters German mountaineering myth a generation before Buhl.

## 1936–1940: mountains in the service of ideology

**Riccardo Cassin** — **1938:** with Gino Esposito and Ugo Tizzoni climbed the Walker Spur (*Sperone Walker*) on the north face of the Grandes Jorasses (4208 m, ~1200 m of wall) — with the Eiger and Matterhorn one of the “three last great problems of the Alps.”

**Vitaly Abalakov** — **1936:** climbed Khan Tengri (7010 m), losing several fingers and part of a foot to frostbite. **1938:** arrested by the NKVD on a charge of “propagating Western climbing technique,” under investigation until 1940.

**[Emilio Comici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilio_Comici)** (Italy) — **1933:** north face of Cima Grande (2999 m) with the Dimai brothers. **1937:** soloed the route in 3 hours 45 minutes. In **1940** died on the training cliffs of Vallunga: testing a prusik cord’s strength, leaning over a ledge, he fell when the cord failed.

**[Heinrich Harrer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Harrer)** (1912–2006), **[Anderl Heckmair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderl_Heckmair)**, **[Fritz Kasparek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Kasparek)**, **[Ludwig Vörg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_V%C3%B6rg)** — **24 July 1938:** first ascent of the Eiger North Face (3967 m, ~1800 m of wall) **[confirmed]**. Harrer was a member of the NSDAP; Nazi propaganda used the success; Hitler received the climbers. Vörg (who also made the first ascent of the west face of Ushba in the Caucasus) would die on the first day of Operation Barbarossa in 1941. Harrer’s later fate: interned by the British in India, he escaped in 1944 to Tibet, where he spent seven years, becoming friend and tutor to the young 14th Dalai Lama — the book *Seven Years in Tibet* (1952) became a world bestseller and a Hollywood film (1997, with Brad Pitt). After the film’s release his Nazi past (SS membership) surfaced, prompting controversy, though he was never implicated in crimes. Died 7 January 2006, aged 93, in Austria.

The context of the 1938 triumph is the tragedy of **1936**: **[Toni Kurz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Eiger_north_face_climbing_disaster)** and Andreas Hinterstoisser died attempting the Eiger North Face; Kurz died before the rescuers’ eyes, hanging on the rope. Without that shadow the “victory” of 1938 reads incomplete.

**[Fritz Wiessner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Wiessner)** (Germany/USA, 1900–1988; *mountaineering / rock*) — leader of the American **1939** K2 expedition. With **Pasang Dawa Lama** he climbed without oxygen to ~8380 m on the Abruzzi Ridge — doing almost all the leading; a few hundred metres short of the summit. The descent and the fate of **Dudley Wolfe**, left higher on the mountain, became one of the first great moral forks of high-altitude mountaineering. The American Alpine Club inquiry issued no clear verdict; the blame fight (Wiessner / Cromwell / Durrance and others) ran for decades. The modern consensus leans toward **shared responsibility** for a chain of communication and logistics failures, not a single villain.

**[Dudley Wolfe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudley_Wolfe)** (USA, 1896–1939; *high-altitude mountaineering*) — American yachtsman and amateur climber, sponsor and member of Wiessner’s **1939** K2 expedition. He had almost no big-altitude experience, yet climbed high and remained at Camp VII (~7300–7500 m) while the leaders pushed for the summit and lower camps were partly **stripped or abandoned** amid confused orders. After Wiessner’s descent Wolfe was left **alone** high on the mountain. Rescue attempts: Sherpas **Pasang Kikuli**, **Pasang Kitar** and **Phinsoo** reached him after roughly a week alone — Wolfe was extremely weak, refused to descend “today,” asked them to return tomorrow; two days later the same three went up again — and **all four vanished**. These were the **first four deaths on K2**. For years Wolfe was reduced to “the inexperienced rich man who was abandoned” — a simplification that erases both his persistence at altitude and the Sherpas who died trying to save him.

**What is known today.** In **2002**, on the Godwin-Austen Glacier, **Jennifer Jordan** and **Jeff Rhoads** found remains beside tent debris (“Made in India”), Cambridge-labeled clothing, and a mitten marked **WOLFE** — identifying Wolfe (AAC / Jordan). Circumstantial evidence suggests he most likely died at or near his high camp; the three Sherpa rescuers’ remains have never been securely confirmed. Memory of Wolfe is not a footnote to the Wiessner ethics debate, but a human story of K2’s first tragedy; beside him in the canon stand the Sherpa rescuers.

## 1941–1945: mountains as a front line

The Battle of the Caucasus 1942–1943: the “Edelweiss” division plants swastika flags on Elbrus (5642 m); in February 1943 Soviet mountaineers take them down in midwinter. Yevgeny Abalakov goes to the front in the first days of the war.

## 1946–1950: French triumph and its price

**[Maurice Herzog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Herzog) (1919–2012) and [Louis Lachenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Lachenal)** (France) — **3 June 1950:** the first eight-thousander in history — Annapurna (8091 m), without oxygen **[confirmed]**. Herzog lost his gloves on the descent, both lost their boots; they spent the night in a snow hole in a blizzard. Herzog lost all fingers and toes, Lachenal his toes. Herzog’s bestseller cast him as the hero and barely mentioned the contribution of **[Lionel Terray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Terray)** (1921–1965, who did not himself reach the summit but with a Sherpa brought down the blinded and frostbitten leaders) and **[Gaston Rébuffat](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A0%D0%B5%D0%B1%D1%8E%D1%84%D1%84%D0%B0,_%D0%93%D0%B0%D1%81%D1%82%D0%BE%D0%BD)** (1921–1985). Lachenal’s diaries were published only in 1996 — with cuts removed in editing involving Herzog; the story is dissected in David Roberts’s *True Summit* (2000). Lachenal died in 1955, falling into a crevasse on skis in Chamonix. Terray, who took part in the first ascent of Makalu (1955) and saved people in dozens of operations, died in 1965 in the mountains; his book *Conquistadors of the Useless* became one of the central texts on mountaineering’s inner logic. Rébuffat climbed all six great north faces of the Alps and for decades was the face of French guiding. Herzog later became France’s minister of sport under de Gaulle and mayor of Chamonix; died in 2012, aged 93.


**[Ivan Antonovich](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%90%D0%BD%D1%82%D0%BE%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%87,_%D0%98%D0%B2%D0%B0%D0%BD_%D0%98%D0%BE%D1%81%D0%B8%D1%84%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%87)** (USSR, 1909–1994; *competition rock climbing*) — **summer 1947:** on the cliffs of Dombay staged the world’s first **official** rock-climbing competitions (rules, programme, prizes) — the point from which Soviet rock climbing separates from mountaineering as a sport in its own right. Later — chief judge of USSR championships, chair of the rock committee; Honoured Coach of the USSR.

**[Vladimir Racek](http://www.alpklubspb.ru/persona/racek.htm)** (USSR, Uzbekistan/Kyrgyzstan, 1918–1980; *high-altitude mountaineering / military topographer*) — one of the founders of mountaineering and mountain tourism in Central Asia; expeditions and first ascents in the Tien Shan and Pamirs; refining the height and position of Peak Pobeda; USSR champion in the high-altitude class (**1950**, mass Uzbek ascent of Peak Lenin). Without Racek the Central Asian line of the Soviet canon reads as “someone else’s” Pamir.

## 1951–1955: Everest, betrayal on K2 and a night without the summit

**[Tenzing Norgay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenzing_Norgay) (1914–1986) and [Edmund Hillary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Hillary) (1919–2008)** — **29 May 1953:** first to the summit of Everest (8849 m) **[confirmed]**. By then Tenzing had taken part in six previous attempts since 1935 and a year earlier, with Raymond Lambert’s Swiss expedition, had climbed higher than anyone in the future British team. Hillary grew up in New Zealand’s Southern Alps (Cook/Aoraki and others); devoted the rest of his life to building schools and hospitals for Nepal’s Sherpas; died in 2008 in Auckland. Tenzing — equal first ascentionist; later — director of field training at the Himalayan Mountaineering Institute in Darjeeling.

**[Nawang Gombu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawang_Gombu_Sherpa)** (Tibet/India, 1936–2011) — Tenzing’s nephew; in 1953 — the team’s junior Sherpa, reached the South Col. **1963:** Everest with Jim Whittaker (first American summit); **20 May 1965:** again with the Indian expedition together with **Avtar Singh Cheema** — **the first person to climb Everest twice**, and simultaneously **the first Indians on the summit** **[confirmed]** (the “twice” record stood ~15–20 years). Later — HMI Darjeeling and guiding on Rainier; Sherpa and Indian agency after Tenzing — already as authors of their own summits, not only as support for others’.

**[Pasang Dawa Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasang_Dawa_Lama)** (Nepal/Sherpa) — key high-altitude Sherpa of the era of the first eight-thousanders (including a role on K2 1954) — Sherpa agency in the canon exists long before the digital age of records.

**[Achille Compagnoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achille_Compagnoni) (1914–2009) and [Lino Lacedelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lino_Lacedelli)** (Italy; *high-altitude mountaineering*) — **31 July 1954:** first ascent of K2 (8611 m) **[confirmed]** — the second eight-thousander in history after Annapurna. The triumph of Ardito Desio’s expedition is forever bound to what happened the night before at the assault camp.

**[Walter Bonatti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Bonatti) (1930–2011) and [Amir Mehdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Mehdi) (1913–1999)** (Italy / Pakistan; *mountaineering (high-altitude)* — Mehdi: Hunza porter who a year earlier had already brought frostbitten Hermann Buhl down from Nanga Parbat):
- **30–31 July 1954:** on expedition orders they carried oxygen cylinders to assault Camp IX for Compagnoni and Lacedelli. The camp proved to have been pitched **far higher** than agreed — reaching the tent in darkness and exhaustion was already nearly impossible.
- Mehdi, unlike the Italians, was **not issued high-altitude boots** — only ordinary army boots two sizes too small. Both spent the night without a tent at about **8100 m**.
- In the morning Compagnoni and Lacedelli took the oxygen and went for the summit; Bonatti and Mehdi descended. Mehdi lost all his toes (amputation); later he became a civil servant in Pakistan and received (according to his son — not always regularly) a pension from Italy.
- For years Compagnoni accused Bonatti of **stealing part of the oxygen**; an Italian court **cleared** Bonatti in **1964**. The Club Alpino Italiano officially recognized his account only in **2008**, 54 years later.
- After 1954 Bonatti was for decades effectively ostracized; in **1965** he left high-altitude mountaineering for journalism. **2009:** first recipient of the newly established career Piolet d'Or. Died of cancer in **2011**, aged 81.
- **1955:** solo first ascent of the south-west buttress of the Petit Dru (Alps, 3733 m) — in large part a personal answer to the betrayal of 1954.


**[Hermann Buhl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Buhl)** (Austria, 1924–1957) — **3 July 1953:** first ascent of Nanga Parbat (8126 m), the “mountain of destiny” of German-language mountaineering that had taken the lives of the 1934 and 1937 expeditions — in defiance of expedition leader Karl Herrligkoffer’s direct order to retreat **[confirmed]**. Partner Otto Kempter turned back, and Buhl reached the summit alone, without radio contact, having taken a stimulant (“Pervitin”) — 41 hours out and back. On the descent he lost his ice axe and a crampon strap, and with nightfall was forced to stand out the night on a narrow ledge at about 7900 m, without tent, sleeping bag or oxygen, holding the rock with one hand. He survived the night and descended to people — among those who carried him was the already mentioned Amir Mehdi. Herrligkoffer publicly called Buhl’s solo push disloyal to the team. Reinhold Messner, who repeated the route solo in 1978, called Buhl’s ascent “fifty years ahead of its time.” In **1957** — in the foursome of the first ascent of Broad Peak (8051 m), without high-altitude porters or oxygen; a few days later he died on Chogolisa, falling through a cornice.

**[Joe Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Brown_(climber))** (United Kingdom, 1930–2020) and **[George Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Band)** — **1955:** Kangchenjunga; Brown — the face of British working-class rockcraft, without whom the line from Wales to the Himalaya is incomplete.

**[Kurt Diemberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Diemberger)** (Austria) — Broad Peak 1957 with Buhl; later Dhaulagiri; witness to K2 tragedies; Piolet d'Or Carrière.

**[Lionel Terray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Terray) and [Guido Magnone](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_Magnone)** — **February 1952:** first ascent of Fitz Roy / Cerro Chaltén (Patagonia) **[confirmed]**. Magnone led the key pitches of the final push; for Terray, already through Annapurna, this opens the Patagonian line of the canon. In the expedition Jacques Poincenot died while fording the Fitz Roy river.

**The Andes enter the canon under their own name:** from this point Patagonia is an independent stage of world history, though local guides and Argentine participants (F. Ibáñez and others) long remain in the shadow of French reportage.

**César Morales Arnao** (Peru, 1929–2022; [El Comercio profile](https://elcomercio.pe/archivo-elcomercio/cesar-morales-arnao-el-episodio-de-primera-vez-que-una-bandera-peruana-flameo-en-la-cima-de-europa-junto-a-un-chullo-y-una-moneda-de-un-sol-mont-blanc-andinista-alpinista-y-periodista-primer-peruano-en-llegar-al-monte-blanco-1961-hace-60-anos-chullu-andino-nnsp-noticia/)) — organizer of the **first Peruvian** expedition to Huascarán (1953); later — co-founder of Huascarán National Park, author of books on Andinism, first Peruvian on Mont Blanc (1961). The Cordillera Blanca enters the canon not as a range for foreigners but as a Peruvian school.

**[Marco Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Cruz)** (Ecuador, b. 1945) — guide and historian of Chimborazo: hundreds (by various counts — on the order of a thousand) of ascents of the country’s high point; pioneer of professional guiding and adventure tourism in Ecuador. Ecuador’s volcanoes enter the canon in a local voice, not only via Whymper’s routes.

**[Alain Mesili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Mesili)** (France/Bolivia, b. 1949) and **Ernesto Sánchez** (Bolivia, †1973) — from the turn of the 1960s–1970s open hard lines in the Cordillera Real (Illampu, Ancohuma, Condoriri, Illimani); Mesili stays in La Paz as guide and instructor for Club Andino Boliviano. Sánchez — the strongest Bolivian mountaineer of his generation — died on Illimani in August 1973 with Pierre Dedieu. Bolivia enters the Andean canon with its own school, not only as a backdrop for European expeditions.


## 1956–1960: birth of bouldering, Chinese Everest from the north — and the first great mystification

**[John Gill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Gill_(climber))** (USA, mathematician by training; *bouldering*) single-handedly sets bouldering apart as a discipline: gymnastic chalk, dynamic movement, the first grading system (B-scale).

**[Cesare Maestri](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9C%D0%B0%D1%8D%D1%81%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B8,_%D0%A7%D0%B5%D0%B7%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B5)** (Italy, 1929–2016) — claimed that in **1959** with Austrian ice climber Toni Egger he made the first ascent of Cerro Torre (3128 m, Patagonia); the only proof, he said, was carried away by an avalanche with the dead Egger. **[not accepted by the community]** as a completed first ascent. In 1970, trying to restore his reputation, Maestri returned with a petrol compressor and drilled hundreds of bolt holes up the ridge (“Compressor Route”), leaving a 150-kilogram compressor on the wall — an act called “desecration of the mountain.” The compressor was removed in 2012; that same **2012** the route was climbed completely free (no aid) by Austrian **[David Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lama)**, who at first drew sharp criticism for a bolt-assisted preparation but returned and closed the line clean — the practical rehabilitation of a spoiled wall.

**[Tom Hornbein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Hornbein) and [Willi Unsoeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willi_Unsoeld)** (USA) — preparation for Everest’s West Ridge; the traverse itself will come in 1963 (see below), but the idea is born in this era of American Himalayanism.

**[Wang Fuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Fuzhou), Qu Yinhua and [Gongbu (Konbu)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gongbu_(mountaineer))** (China / Tibet) — **25 May 1960:** first ascent of Everest from the north (North-East Ridge) and first Chinese/Tibetan on the summit **[confirmed]** by most sources; some Western mountaineers long **[disputed]** the claim for lack of summit photos and the story of the Second Step (Qu Yinhua supposedly removed his boots and stood on Liu Lianman’s shoulders). The PRC state expedition — a parallel to the Soviet model: the mountain as national project.


## 1961–1965: a bar ahead of its time

**John Gill** — **1961:** unroped solo of the overhanging Thimble Overhang (Needles, South Dakota). Consensus grade today — about **5.12a / V4–V5** (highball; on the French sport scale ≈ **7a+**; this is *not* Font 7A+, which corresponds roughly to V8). For its era the line was almost unthinkable; not repeated in the original style for more than 20 years (around 1987).

**[Royal Robbins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Robbins) (1935–2017)** (USA) saw climbing as a form of self-expression, not conquest, and deliberately minimized bolts — in contrast to the style of **[Warren Harding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Harding_(climber))** (Nose, 1958 — the siege of El Capitan). Admitted that attempts to repeat Gill’s Thimble Overhang were his “greatest defeat.”
- **1961:** Salathé Wall on El Capitan (~900 m).

**Hornbein and Unsoeld** — **1963:** Everest West Ridge and full traverse of the massif — one of the greatest high-altitude achievements of the twentieth century **[confirmed]**.

**[Chris Bonington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Bonington)** (United Kingdom) begins the era of large British Himalayan sieges that will later yield Everest’s SW Face.

**Wang Fuzhou** and the Chinese team — **1964:** first ascent of Shishapangma (8027 m) — the last unclimbed eight-thousander **[confirmed]**. The Tibetan–Chinese high-altitude school consolidates as an independent line beside British and Swiss Himalayanism.


## 1966–1970: tiger of the rocks, Saxon recluse and a revolution in ice

**[Mikhail Khergiani](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Хергиани,_Михаил_Виссарионович)** (Georgia, USSR; *competition rock climbing / mountaineering*) — “Tiger of the Rocks,” seven-time USSR climbing champion, son of one of Svaneti’s first mountaineers, Vissarion Khergiani (who had climbed Ushba as early as 1937). Died **4 July 1969** on the Su Alto plateau in the Dolomites (Italy): a rockfall cut the belay rope; the fall — about 600 metres.

**[Yuichiro Miura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuichiro_Miura)** (Japan, b. 1932) — **6 May 1970:** first ski descent of Everest (from the South Col, with a parachute for braking) **[confirmed]**; the film *The Man Who Skied Down Everest* — Academy Award for documentary. Later — oldest on the summit of Everest at **80** (**2013**, with son Gota) **[confirmed]** (some chroniclers **[dispute]** the completeness of style because of oxygen/support). The Japanese canon — not only expeditions and Uemura’s solos, but speed/age as a separate axis.

**[Bernd Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernd_Arnold)** (GDR, b. 1947) — already at 19, in 1966, climbed “Zehn” (VIIIc) on Meurererturm; begins a series of more than 900 first ascents in Saxon Switzerland, often barefoot: local rules categorically forbid metal protection. Later — member of the first ascent of Riders on the Storm (1991).

**[Yvon Chouinard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvon_Chouinard)** (USA, b. 1938) — son of French immigrants, started at the forge: from the late 1950s forged his own steel pitons for Yosemite. **1966:** developed ice tools with a curved, not straight, pick — a technical revolution that first made steep ice reliably climbable. Later — essay and practice of “clean climbing,” founder of Patagonia; in 2022 transferred the company to a trust for climate goals.

**[Naomi Uemura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naomi_Uemura)** — **1970:** solo on Everest — first Japanese to climb alone **[confirmed]**.

## 1971–1975: eight women, the Cozzolino dihedral and the “red point”

**[Elvira Shatayeva](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A8%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B0%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%B0,_%D0%AD%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%80%D0%B0_%D0%A1%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B3%D0%B5%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%BD%D0%B0) and her seven partners** (USSR) — in August **1974** the first all-women team climbed Lenin Peak (7134 m); the ascent was conceived as a demonstration that women could manage a technically hard high-altitude *traverse* on equal terms with men (the full massif traverse was not completed). On the descent a severe storm caught them; contact with base camp, coordinated in part by Vitaly Abalakov, was maintained almost to the end. Shatayeva refused to abandon the sick (“we will not leave comrades after all they have done for us”). All eight women died (7–8 August): Tatyana Bardasheva, Nina Vasilyeva, Irina Lyubimtseva, Lyudmila Manzharova, Ilsiyar Mukhamedova, Galina Perekhodyuk, Valentina Fateyeva and Elvira Shatayeva.

**[Junko Tabei](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A2%D0%B0%D0%B1%D1%8D%D0%B8,_%D0%94%D0%B7%D1%8E%D0%BD%D0%BA%D0%BE)** (Japan, 1939–2016) — founder of the Japanese women’s alpine club; assembled and led the first all-women Himalayan expedition. Twelve days before the summit an avalanche buried her team’s Everest camp, covering Tabei; Sherpas dug her out, but she refused evacuation. **16 May 1975:** became the first woman to climb Everest **[confirmed]**. In 1992 the first woman to complete the Seven Summits. Author and activist; almost simultaneously with the women’s tragedy on Lenin Peak her Everest became another outcome of the same historical question about women’s right to altitude. Died in 2016, aged 77, of cancer — still climbing during treatment.

**[Pan Duo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Duo)** (also Phantog; China/Tibet, 1939–2014) — **27 May 1975:** second woman on Everest and first to climb from the **northern** (Tibetan) side **[confirmed]** — almost simultaneously with Tabei, but by another state machine and another flank. Together they close “women’s Everest” 1975 from both sides of the ridge.

**[Xia Boyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xia_Boyu)** (China, b. 1949) — member of the Chinese Everest expedition **1975**; gave his sleeping bag to a partner in a storm, lost both feet to frostbite, later — below-knee amputations after lymphoma. **14 May 2018**, aged 69, climbed Everest on prostheses **[confirmed]** (fifth attempt; Laureus Sporting Moment 2019). Chinese high-altitude history — not only the state flags of 1960/1975, but personal stubbornness across decades.

**[Ian Howell](https://www.mck.or.ke/in-memoriam/ian-howell-tribute-page/) and Ian Allan** (United Kingdom/Kenya) — from the 1970s systematically develop East African rock: Hell’s Gate, Lukenya, Nelion/Batian on Mount Kenya. Howell alone built a bivouac hut on Nelion (about 13 solo ascents in a few weeks of 1970). The African *rock* canon begins not only with Rocklands — Kenya supplies its own trad and alpine school decades earlier.

**Todra Gorge** (High Atlas, Morocco) — North African limestone pole: first technical lines from **1966**; Spanish bolts in 1976; in winter **1987** a Dutch wave installs ~120 sport routes and makes the gorge a European “winter” destination. **1997:** Lynn Hill climbs Tête de Chou (8b) — one of the area’s ceilings. Africa in the canon — not only Rocklands and Kenya, but Atlas sport climbing as a bridge to Europe.


**[Enzo Cozzolino](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzo_Cozzolino)**, nicknamed **“Grongo”** (Italy, Trieste, 1948–1972) — one of the first masters of extremely bold free climbing with a minimum of pitons.
- **22–23 September 1970:** with Armando Bernardini — the 800-metre north dihedral on Piccolo Mangart di Coritenza (Julian Alps), now “Diedro Cozzolino,” climbed almost free (~10 pitons for the whole wall).
- Died **18 June 1972** soloing Torre di Babele (Civetta group), aged 23. Messner called him one of the best rock climbers he had ever seen.

**[Kurt Albert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Albert)** (Germany, 1954–2010) — at 17 climbed the Walker Spur, a year later the Eiger North Face, and in the mid-1970s in the Frankenjura began marking with a red point routes climbed entirely free. German “Rotpunkt” gave English “redpoint” — today a basic concept of sport climbing. In 1991 with Bernd Arnold and Wolfgang Güllich made the first ascent of Riders on the Storm — a 1300-metre route in Patagonia long considered one of the world’s hardest big walls. Died 28 September 2010: fell 18 metres while photographing from a via ferrata in Bavaria; died two days later in hospital.

**[Jim Bridwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Bridwell)**, nicknamed “The Bird” (USA, 1944–2018) — central figure of Yosemite’s Camp 4 in the seventies, inventor of copperhead protection. With John Long and Billy Westbay — **1975:** first one-day ascent of The Nose on El Capitan. Died in 2018 from complications of an infected tattoo done in Borneo — in its way absurd, in its way logical ending for a life in which the line between adventure and risk was never sharp.

**[Yvon Chouinard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvon_Chouinard)** — **1972:** alarmed that the hammering and removal of steel pitons (including those made by his own company — then 70% of its revenue) was irreversibly destroying Yosemite cracks, with partner Tom Frost and essayist Doug Robinson issued a catalogue urging abandonment of pitons for removable protection — the start of the “clean climbing” movement, voluntarily collapsing the main income source of his own company. Later Patagonia and Black Diamond grew from that base.

**[Doug Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Scott)** (United Kingdom, 1941–2020) — **1975:** with Dougal Haston — Everest South-West Face on Bonington’s expedition; later Baintha Brakk and Piolet Carrière; humanitarian Community Action Nepal.

## 1976–1980: without oxygen, first Pakistani on K2, first winter Everest

**[Reinhold Messner](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9C%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%81%D0%BD%D0%B5%D1%80,_%D0%A0%D0%B0%D0%B9%D0%BD%D1%85%D0%BE%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%B4) (b. 1944, Italy — South Tyrol; *high-altitude mountaineering*) and [Peter Habeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Habeler) (b. 1942, Austria; *high-altitude mountaineering*)** — **8 May 1978:** first to climb Everest without oxygen **[confirmed]**. Messner — Italian citizen from German-speaking South Tyrol. Earlier with Habeler: speed Eiger (1974, ~10 hours) and Andean ascents (incl. Yerupajá); south face of Aconcagua (1974, partly a new route). Later: solo Everest (1980), first person on all 14 eight-thousanders without oxygen (1986); **1989–1990:** Antarctic crossing on foot via the South Pole with Arved Fuchs (~2800 km); Messner Mountain Museum; co-founder of Mountain Wilderness. **In 2017**, aged ~74–75, Habeler repeated the Eiger North Face with David Lama; in **2026** he received the Piolet d'Or Carrière for lifetime contribution to mountaineering.

**[Ashraf Aman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashraf_Aman)** (Pakistan, b. **15 January 1938**) — **9 August 1977:** first Pakistani on K2 (Japan–Pakistan expedition) **[confirmed]**. Earlier — “Himalayan Tiger” on Nanga Parbat (1962). With Mehdi, Sabir, Sadpara and others he sets a Pakistani subject line in which there are not only dead porters but authors of summits.

**[Nazir Sabir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazir_Sabir)** (Pakistan) — first Pakistani on Everest (1981); later — politician and organizer. With Aman he sets the Pakistani subject line of high-altitude mountaineering.

**[Andrzej Zawada](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrzej_Zawada)** (Poland, 1928–2000) — architect of Polish winter Himalayanism, geophysicist by training: organized the 1979–1980 expedition in which **[Krzysztof Wielicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krzysztof_Wielicki) and [Leszek Cichy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leszek_Cichy) (b. 1951)** on **17 February 1980** made the first winter ascent of an eight-thousander in history — Everest **[confirmed]**. From the summit they radioed base: “Hello, base? Do you hear us?” Cichy later became the first Pole to complete the Seven Summits and went into finance. Zawada died in 2000 in Warsaw.

**[Ray Jardine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Jardine)** (USA) — January **1978:** patented spring-loaded camming devices (“Friends”), developing Greg Lowe’s earlier idea (1973 patent) of a constant camming angle.

**[John Bachar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Bachar)** (USA, 1957–2009) — icon of free solo in Yosemite and “ground-up” ethics; Bachar-Yerian; died in 2009 soloing near home in Mammoth Lakes.

**[Wanda Rutkiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanda_Rutkiewicz) (1943–1992)** (Poland) — **1978:** first European woman on Everest; the next decade she devoted to independent women’s mountaineering.


## 1981–1985: Italian purism, Polish gold, Soviet Everest and the Shining Wall

**[Vladimir Balyberdin](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Балыбердин,_Владимир_Сергеевич)** (USSR, Leningrad, 1949–1994; *high-altitude mountaineering*) and **Eduard Myslovsky** (USSR; *high-altitude mountaineering*) — **4 May 1982:** the first Soviet mountaineers on Everest (as a rope team). The first Soviet Himalayan expedition opened a new route on the south-west face. On the **summit push from Camp V** Balyberdin climbed **without a mask**; he used oxygen **the night before the push** (minimal flow) and **partly on the descent**. Myslovsky climbed with oxygen. Two other members reached the summit at night. Died **22 July 1994** in St Petersburg, hit by a truck that ran a red light.

**[Sergey Bershov](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%91%D0%B5%D1%80%D1%88%D0%BE%D0%B2,_%D0%A1%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B3%D0%B5%D0%B9_%D0%98%D0%B3%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%87)** and **[Mikhail Turkevich](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A2%D1%83%D1%80%D0%BA%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%87,_%D0%9C%D0%B8%D1%85%D0%B0%D0%B8%D0%BB_%D0%9C%D0%B8%D1%85%D0%B0%D0%B9%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%87)** — night Everest 1982 and later rescue operations; without them the Soviet Himalayan chapter looks one-sided. **[Kazbek Valiyev](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%92%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%B8%D0%B5%D0%B2,_%D0%9A%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%BA_%D0%9C%D0%B0%D0%B3%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%B5%D0%B4%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%87)** — Everest ’82; later the Kazakh school from which Urubko grew.

**[Valery Khrishchaty](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A5%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%89%D0%B0%D1%82%D1%8B%D0%B9,_%D0%92%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B9_%D0%90%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%BA%D1%81%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B4%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%87)** (USSR/Kazakhstan, 1951–1993) — oxygenless high-altitude climber of the Soviet school; the hardest traverses of the Pamirs and Tien Shan. Died **4 August 1993** in an avalanche on Khan Tengri.

**[Maurizio Zanolla, “Manolo”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurizio_Zanolla)** (Italy, b. 1958) — famous for free-solo ascents in the Dolomites, never competing on principle.
- **1981:** first ascent of Il Mattino dei Maghi (Totoga, Dolomites), grade 7c+.
- **1986:** l’Ultimo Movimento (Totoga) — one of the first 8b routes in Italy; **1992:** one of the first Italian free solos of 8a (Masala Dosa).
- **2008:** at 49 repeated Fred Nicole’s Bimbaluna (9a+) — a rare example of elite level near 50.

**[Yuri Mamleyev](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9C%D0%B0%D0%BC%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%B5%D0%B2,_%D0%AE%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B9_%D0%92%D0%B8%D1%82%D0%B0%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%87)** (USSR/Russia; *rock climbing (sport/trad)*) — one of the key figures of Soviet/Russian rock climbing (**Crimea**, Nikita Gorge and others); multiple champion, coach of generations. With the Krasnoyarsk **stolbists** and the Khergiani school he closes the line “rock as a discipline in its own right” inside the Soviet canon — beside altitude, not instead of it.

**[Valery Balezin](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%91%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%B7%D0%B8%D0%BD,_%D0%92%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%B5%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%B9_%D0%92%D0%B8%D0%BA%D1%82%D0%BE%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%87)** (USSR/Russia, b. 1953; *competition rock climbing / competition alpinism*) — Krasnoyarsk school: the first in the USSR to earn **Master of Sport, International Class in rock climbing** (1982), multiple USSR champion; one of the few also MSMK in mountaineering. Beside him in the Soviet comps canon — **Flyura Zhirnova** and other champions of the 1970s–80s: without that row “rock climbing as sport” looks like a Western invention of the 1980s alone.

**Khibiny and Karelia** (USSR/Russia) — northern rock–alpine contour: the Kirovsk section from the mid-1960s (Yuri Tovstenko and Leningrad Mining graduates); from **1992** — systematic classification of Khibiny routes (Alexander Proskura, Oleg Shumilov, Yuri Serebryakov and others); Karelian **Bolshiye Skaly / Yastrebinoe** — training ground of the Leningrad school from the 1950s–1960s. Not “small mountains instead of the Himalaya,” but a separate geography of the Soviet/Russian canon.

**[Jerzy Kukuczka](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9A%D1%83%D0%BA%D1%83%D1%87%D0%BA%D0%B0,_%D0%95%D0%B6%D0%B8)** (Poland, 1948–1989) — symbol of the “golden age” of Polish Himalayanism: poverty, homemade gear, new routes where others used already fixed lines.
- **21 January 1985:** with Andrzej Czok — first winter ascent of Dhaulagiri (8167 m).
- **1986:** new route on the south face of K2 with Tadeusz Piotrowski.
- **18 September 1987:** second person to climb all 14 eight-thousanders — almost all by new routes or in winter.
- Died **24 October 1989** on the south face of Lhotse: an old rope he was holding near the summit broke.

**[Wojciech Kurtyka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojciech_Kurtyka) (b. 1947) and [Robert Schauer](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Schauer)** (Poland/Austria) — already in 1973 Kurtyka made the first winter ascent of Trollveggen (Norway) — the continent’s largest vertical wall. Earlier — new lines on the Gasherbrums and an ethic of refusing siege; Schauer later became a director (*The Dark Glow of the Mountains* and others).
- **13–20 July 1985:** west face of Gasherbrum IV (7925 m, wall ~2500 m) — the “Shining Wall” **[confirmed]** as one of the greatest alpine achievements of the century; the route long went unrepeated. They climbed in pure alpine style; caught in snowfall and stretching the ascent, after reaching the ridge they refused the short traverse to the true summit and began the descent at once. Kurtyka called his philosophy “the art of suffering”; in 2016 he received the Piolet d'Or for lifetime contribution to mountaineering.

**[Patrick Edlinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Edlinger)** (France, 1960–2012) — the TV film *La Vie au bout des doigts* (1982) showed his free soloing in the Verdon and Buoux — possibly the first climber with genuine mass media fame.

**[Catherine Destivelle](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%94%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B8%D0%B2%D0%B5%D0%BB%D1%8C,_%D0%9A%D0%B0%D1%82%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%BD)** (France) — strongest competition climber of the mid-1980s (Rock Master and others); **1991:** Voie Destivelle on the Petit Dru — first woman to open an extreme alpine route. Winter solo trilogy of the north faces: Eiger (**1992**, Heckmair), Grandes Jorasses / Walker (**1993**), Matterhorn / Bonatti (**1994**) **[confirmed]** — first woman to close it. A bridge from comps into big alpine climbing before the Olympic era. In **2020** the first woman to receive the career Piolet d'Or.

**[Bachendri Pal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachendri_Pal)** (India, b. 1954) — **23 May 1984:** first Indian woman on Everest **[confirmed]** (Everest ’84 expedition; survived an avalanche in camp and continued the push). Later — director of Tata Steel Adventure Foundation, leader of women’s expeditions (incl. 1993). The Indian women’s high-altitude canon after Gombu/Cheema steps out of the shadow of an “only male army machine.”

**[Harish Kapadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harish_Kapadia)** (India, b. 1945) — exploratory pole of Indian Himalayanism: dozens of first ascents (Devtoli 6788 m and others), opening “unknown valleys” of Garhwal, Spiti, Eastern Karakoram; long-time editor of the *Himalayan Journal*; RGS Patron’s Medal, Tenzing Norgay Award, Piolets d'Or Asia Lifetime (2017). India in the canon — not only army Everest, but reconnaissance of its own ranges.

**[Tim Macartney-Snape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Macartney-Snape) and [Greg Mortimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Mortimer)** (Australia) — **3 October 1984:** first Australians on Everest, without oxygen, by a new route on the north face (“White Limbo” / Norton Couloir) **[confirmed]**. Macartney-Snape later — first person from sea to Everest summit on foot (**1990**, Sea to Summit). The Australian high-altitude canon enters beside NZ Hillary — already by its own route, not a repeat of the classic.

## 1986–1990: first 8c, Karo’s Patagonian walls, speed Everest and a disputed Lhotse solo

**[Wolfgang Güllich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_G%C3%BCllich) (1960–1992)** (Germany) — **1987:** Wallstreet (Frankenjura) — the world’s first 8c, after Kanal im Rücken (1984, first 8b) and Punks in the Gym (1985, first 8b+). For Wallstreet training he invented the campus board — a tool that later became standard in every climbing gym worldwide.

**[Wanda Rutkiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanda_Rutkiewicz)** — **1986:** first woman to climb K2 **[confirmed]**. Died in **1992** on Kangchenjunga (8586 m) — by the main account, caught in a snowstorm near the summit; body never found.

**[Erhard Loretan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erhard_Loretan) (1959–2011) and [Jean Troillet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Troillet)** (Switzerland) — **1986:** climbed the North Face of Everest without oxygen and without a tent, descending on skis and by glissading in less than a day after leaving 6500 m — a model of ultralight style. In 1995 Loretan became the third person to complete all 14 eight-thousanders. Died in 2011, falling from a cliff in the Swiss Alps.

**[Tomo Česen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomo_%C4%8Cesen)** (Slovenia) — claimed a solo of Lhotse’s south face, April **1990**; serious inconsistencies in the account leave the ascent unproven **[disputed]**; there was also no official community recognition of proven falsification.

**[Fred Nicole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Nicole)** (Switzerland, b. 1970; *bouldering*) — in the late 1980s–1990s successively raises the bouldering ceiling: first recognized **8B / 8B+ / 8C**; quiet architect of the discipline beside Gill, without a media machine.

**[Silvo Karo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvo_Karo)** (Slovenia) with Janez Jeglič — **1986–1988:** a series of Patagonian walls, culminating in the south face of Cerro Torre (to the junction with Maestri’s line, January 1988) **[confirmed]** as one of the hardest big walls of the era; Jim Bridwell called it the hardest route yet climbed in Patagonia. Later — Piolet d'Or Carrière. The Slovenian school beside Česen — with a different, better-documented trail.


**[Jerry Moffatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Moffatt)** and **[Ben Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Moon)** (United Kingdom) — the British training revolution and the context of Punks in the Gym; without them Güllich sits in a vacuum.

**[Lydia Bradey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia_Bradey)** (New Zealand, b. 1961) — **14 October 1988:** first woman on Everest **without oxygen** **[confirmed]** by the Himalayan Database and later consensus; the claim was at first **[disputed]** by part of the team (Hall/Ball) and complicated by lack of a route permit. Later — multiple ascents and guiding. NZ after Hillary gains a women’s oxygenless frontier.

**[Greg Child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greg_Child)** (Australia, b. 1957) — **1986:** north-west ridge of Gasherbrum IV with Macartney-Snape and Tom Hargis — second ascent of the peak after the Italians of 1958 **[confirmed]**; Yosemite ↔ Karakoram bridge; author of *Thin Air* / *Postcards from the Ledge*. Australian technical climbing beside “White Limbo.”

## 1991–1995: the ninth grade, Pasang Lhamu and a summit at the cost of motherhood

**[Wolfgang Güllich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfgang_G%C3%BCllich)** — **14 September 1991:** Action Directe — the world’s first 9a **[confirmed]**. Died in a car accident in 1992.

**[Beat Kammerlander](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beat_Kammerlander) (b. 1959)** (Austria) — certified mountain and ski guide; transferred competition-level difficulty onto huge alpine walls, establishing routes from the ground up. **1991:** “Unendliche Geschichte” (Rätikon) — the first alpine route of UIAA grade X in history. **1994:** Silbergeier (Rätikon, 8b+) — with “Unendliche Geschichte” for years formed the “trilogy” of the world’s hardest alpine routes, until Alexander Huber surpassed it in 2001 with Bellavista.

**[Fred Rouhling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Rouhling)** (France) — claimed in **1995** the first ascent of Akira at 9b; the community treated the claim with deep distrust **[disputed]** as to the stated grade. The route was repeated only in 2020 by Sébastien Bouin and Lucien Martinez, who graded it 9a — a colossal figure for 1995, but not the one Rouhling himself defended. Bouin also confirmed at 9a two other Rouhling routes (Hugh and L’autre côté du Ciel) — not a fantasist, but a man systematically underrated in his lifetime outside a narrow circle.

**[Lynn Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynn_Hill)** (USA; *rock climbing (sport/trad) / comps*) — before the Nose already dominated comps (Rock Master and others) and outdoor; **1993:** first free ascent of The Nose on El Capitan **[confirmed]**; in **1994** repeated it free in a day; **1997** — Tête de Chou (8b) in Todra. A frontier not only for women’s but for world climbing; memoir *Climbing Free* — one of the discipline’s central texts.

**[Alison Hargreaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alison_Hargreaves)** (United Kingdom) — raising two children in a family that had just lost home and business, and feeding the family by climbing. **1993:** first person, regardless of sex, to solo all six great north faces of the Alps in a single season — including the Eiger in the sixth month of pregnancy. **13 May 1995:** first woman to climb Everest solo, without oxygen and without outside help **[confirmed]**. Died **13 August 1995**, three months later, descending K2 — blown off by a storm. After her death the press discussed not the climb but whether a mother had the right to take such risks — a question almost never put to fathers who die in the mountains. Son Tom Ballard in 2014–2015 would repeat all six faces in winter — and die in 2019 on Nanga Parbat.

**[Pasang Lhamu Sherpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasang_Lhamu_Sherpa)** (Nepal, 1961–1993) — **22 April 1993:** first Nepali and Sherpa woman on Everest **[confirmed]**; died on the descent, staying with a sick partner near the South Summit. National heroine of Nepal; her story makes Sherpa women’s agency visible, not only the labour of supporting others’ expeditions.

**[Santosh Yadav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santosh_Yadav)** (India, b. 1967) — **1992 and 1993:** first woman in the world to climb Everest twice **[confirmed]** (the second time — on a women’s Indo-Nepalese expedition led by Bachendri Pal); later — Kangshung Face. The Indian women’s line beside Pasang Lhamu — another outcome of the same decade.

**[Ang Rita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ang_Rita)** (Nepal, 1948–2020) — “Snow Leopard”: by the mid-1990s — record holder for Everest ascents **without oxygen** (10 times). The labouring Sherpa pole that Kami Rita will later continue.

**[Babu Chiri Sherpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babu_Chiri_Sherpa)** (Nepal, 1965–2001) — speed and endurance records on Everest (incl. a night on the summit, 1999); died in a crevasse on Everest in 2001. Shows that Sherpa history is not only “how many times he climbed,” but own experiments with style.


**[Yasushi Yamanoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasushi_Yamanoi)** (Japan) — solo and alpine style in the Himalaya and Karakoram (incl. lines with Taeko Yamanoi); later Piolet Carrière; a Japanese “quiet” alternative to expedition sieges and media records — beside Taniguchi/Hiraide, but in a more solitary register.

## 1996–2000: dispute over the survivors, a disguised criminal and a painful exit from the stage

The Everest tragedy of May 1996 produced one of the best-known ethical disputes in mountaineering history. Guide **[Anatoli Boukreev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoli_Boukreev)** (USSR/Kazakhstan, born 1958 in Korkino, as a youth recovered from severe asthma; *high-altitude mountaineering*) by the time of the tragedy had already completed the “Snow Leopard” (all seven-thousanders of the USSR, 1985) and climbed 10 of 14 eight-thousanders (18 successful ascents in all) exclusively without oxygen. Journalist Jon Krakauer in the bestseller *Into Thin Air* criticized his tactics; Boukreev and G. DeWalt answered with *The Climb*, noting that it was he who alone, at night, in a storm, brought several lost clients out alive. In December 1997 the American Alpine Club awarded him the David A. Sowles Memorial Award — for saving lives at risk to his own. Died 25 December 1997 with Kazakh cameraman Dmitry Sobolev in an avalanche on winter Annapurna.

**[Rob Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Hall)** (New Zealand, 1961–1996) — co-founder of Adventure Consultants; died on Everest in the storm of 10–11 May 1996, remaining in contact with base near the South Summit. With Gary Ball — the commercial NZ circuit that made guiding on eight-thousanders an industry; 1996 — the price of that model, beside the Boukreev dispute.

**[Henry Todd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Todd_(mountaineer))** (United Kingdom, 1945–2025) — a real criminal past: in the 1970s he ran one of Britain’s largest underground LSD production networks, served 7 of a 13-year sentence after Operation Julia. After release, from 1995, he became one of the pioneers of commercial Everest organization (“Toddfather” of base camp). Died **3 November 2025** in Kathmandu.

**[Alexander Huber](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A5%D1%83%D0%B1%D0%B5%D1%80,_%D0%90%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%BA%D1%81%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B4%D1%80)** (Germany) — with brother Thomas (“Huberbuam”) set the standard for free climbing on big walls. **1996:** Open Air, originally ~9a, later upgraded by the community (incl. Adam Ondra’s assessment) to 9a+. **2001:** Bellavista (north face of Cima di Lavaredo) — surpassed the Kammerlander/Güllich “trilogy” as the world’s hardest alpine route.

**[Katie Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Brown_(climber))** (USA) — dominated competitions in the mid-1990s (Junior World, Rock Master, X-Games, US Nationals), left the sport around 1999. In the memoir *Unraveled* (2022) she wrote openly that the cause was a harsh family situation and an eating disorder.

**[Chris Sharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Sharma)** (USA; *sport climbing / DWS*) — at the turn of the century becomes the face of commercial sport climbing: Realization/Biographie — first consensus **9a+**; Jumbo Love and others; deep-water solo and the media aesthetic of the route as product. The necessary “money and media pole” beside the ascetics — without him the transition to Ondra/Garnbret reads differently.

**[Josune Bereziartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josune_Bereziartu)** (Basque Country, Spain) — **1998–2002:** successively first woman on **8c / 8c+ / 9a / 9a+** **[confirmed]**; before her the women’s sport ceiling lagged the men’s by whole grades.


**[Valery Rozov](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/Розов,_Валерий_Владимирович)** (USSR/Russia, 1964–2017) begins the shift from classic mountaineering (Master of Sport of the USSR) to parachuting and BASE jumping — two-time world parachuting champion (1999, 2003), Russian mountaineering champion (2002, 2004). In 2013 jumped from Everest; in October 2016 set a world BASE-jump altitude record — from the summit of Cho Oyu (7700 m), 90 seconds of freefall. Died 11 November 2017 in the Himalaya, crashing on a wingsuit jump from Ama Dablam.

**[Lhakpa Sherpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lhakpa_Sherpa)** (Nepal, b. 1973) — **2000:** first Nepali woman to climb Everest *and descend successfully*; later — women’s record holder for number of ascents.

**[Carlos Carsolio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Carsolio)** (Mexico) — in the 1990s completes 14×8000 (fourth in the world and first from Latin America on the full list in his generation); speed and solo lines on eight-thousanders. A Mexican high-altitude voice beside the Andean.

**El Potrero Chico** (Nuevo León, Mexico) — from the early 1990s limestone multipitch sport walls (Jeff Jackson, Kevin Gallagher, Kurt Smith, **Ed “Magic” Wright**, local **Ismael Garza** and others): Time Wave Zero, Estrellita, El Sendero Luminoso. Latin America enters the world rock canon not only through Patagonia and Carsolio’s altitude, but through “bolted big walls” near Hidalgo — a range for thousands of visits a year.

**[Um Hong-gil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Um_Hong-gil)** (South Korea) — one of the first Koreans on the full 14×8000 list (finished in 2000); face of the Korean high-altitude school before Park Young-seok and Kim Chang-ho.

**[Li Zhixin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Zhixin) and Wang Yongfeng** (China) — **23 June 1999:** first Chinese to complete the Seven Summits **[confirmed]** (finale — Puncak Jaya / Carstensz Pyramid). The PRC’s civilian high-altitude contour beside the state expeditions of 1960/1975: not only flags on Everest, but the world “seven” as a personal project.


## 2001–2005: rock anarchists, first winter eight-thousanders and Africa on Everest

**[Dean Potter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Potter) (1972–2015)** (USA) — free solo and illegal BASE; after an illegal jump from Delicate Arch he lost Patagonia sponsorship (with then-wife **[Steph Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steph_Davis)** — climber, BASE jumper and author; herself — free solos and BASE). In 2008 climbed a 5.12 route on the Eiger North Face with a single parachute on his back — “freeBASE.” Died 16 May 2015 in Yosemite on a failed wingsuit jump he had done more than 20 times.
- **2002:** speed link of The Nose + Free Rider on El Capitan in under a day — alone.

**[Didier Berthod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Berthod)** (Switzerland) — master of crack climbing (Greenspit, 8b+, first climbed with bolts in 2003, then repeated without them in 2005). In **2005** came close to the first free ascent of Cobra Crack (Squamish, Canada). In 2006, after a knee injury and personal crisis, left climbing and entered a Franciscan monastic community in Switzerland for almost 13 years.

**[Simone Moro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simone_Moro)** (Italy; *high-altitude mountaineering (winter)*) — **2005:** first winter ascent of Shishapangma — the first of his four winter first ascents of eight-thousanders (then Makalu 2009, Gasherbrum II 2011, Nanga Parbat 2016). In 2023, already as a helicopter pilot, took part in rescue operations on Everest.

**[Denis Urubko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Urubko)** (Kazakhstan/Russia; *high-altitude mountaineering*) — in 2001–2006 climbed a series of eight-thousanders without oxygen, including a speed ascent of Gasherbrum II (7.5 hours from 5800 m) and the first ascent of Broad Peak’s south-west face with Sergey Samoilov (2005). Later repeatedly took part in high-altitude rescues, including Nanga Parbat; completed 14×8000.

**[Steve House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_House)** (USA) with Vince Anderson — **2005:** new route on the Rupal Face of Nanga Parbat in alpine style, Piolet d'Or — manifesto of the “small team” against siege.

**Juan Pablo Ruiz Soto** (Colombia, 1957–2023; [El Espectador obituary](https://www.elespectador.com/ambiente/juan-pablo-ruiz-el-inolvidable-banquero-de-las-cimas/)) — with **Cristóbal von Rothkirch** **1988:** first integral link of the Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta summits (Colón–Bolívar–Santander) **[confirmed]**; **22 May 2001** — leader and member of the first successful Colombian Everest expedition (north ridge) **[confirmed]**; programme “Glaciares y Volcanes de Colombia.” The northern Andes and Caribbean Sierra enter the canon in a Colombian voice.

**[José Antonio Delgado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Antonio_Delgado)** (Venezuela, 1965–2006) — “El Indio”: first Venezuelan on five eight-thousanders; led the first Venezuelan Everest expedition (**23 May 2001**, with Marcus Tobía). Speed records on Aconcagua and Huascarán; paraglides from Pico Bolívar and Roraima. Died on Nanga Parbat **22 July 2006**. The Venezuelan high-altitude line — through Mérida and the Sierra Nevada, not through someone else’s canon.

**[Jaime Viñals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaime_Vi%C3%B1als)** (Guatemala, b. 1966) — **23 May 2001:** first person from Central America and the Caribbean on Everest (north ridge) **[confirmed]**; Seven Summits; later — Seven Volcanic Summits and “7+7+7.” The Central American canon enters high-altitude history under its own name, not only as a backdrop for others’ transit.

**Warner Rojas** (Costa Rica) — **May 2012:** first Costa Rican on Everest; **Ligia Madrigal** — **May 2024:** first Costa Rican woman **[confirmed]** (Tico Times / local chronicles). Central America after Viñals gains national women’s and men’s first lines.

**[Sibusiso Vilane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibusiso_Vilane)** (Eswatini/South Africa, b. 1970) — **26 May 2003:** first Black African on Everest **[confirmed]**; later — repeat from the north (2005), Seven Summits, polar treks. The African line enters the high-altitude canon under its own name.

**[Park Young-seok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Young-seok)** (South Korea, 1963–2011) — **2005:** first to complete the True Explorers Grand Slam (14×8000 + Seven Summits + both poles) **[confirmed]**; died in October 2011 on Annapurna. The Korean high-altitude machine reaches world record level.

**Ed February** (South Africa; [Climb ZA](https://www.climbing.co.za/)) and a generation of local first ascentionists of Table Mountain / Cederberg — parallel to Vilane’s altitude develop the *rock* South African canon: routes and access ethics on which Rocklands and Scott Noy’s guides later grow.

**Andy de Klerk** and the Cape Rock generation (South Africa) — hard trad and big-wall lines on Table Mountain and in the Cederberg in the 1980s–1990s: African rockcraft outside the Rocklands bouldering boom on which the international scene later built.


**[Cathy O’Dowd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathy_O%27Dowd)** (South Africa) — first woman to climb Everest from both sides (south and north ridges); key figure of South African women’s high-altitude mountaineering.

**[Apa Sherpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apa_Sherpa)** (Nepal) — multiple record holder for Everest ascents in the “pre–Kami Rita” era; face of the professional Sherpa circuit.

**[Phurba Tashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phurba_Tashi)** (Nepal) — another record holder for Everest summits of the same generation; with Apa he sets the “labour” record contour that Kami Rita will later take over.

**Ciren Duoji (Tsering Dorje / Ciren Duoji)** (Tibet/China, b. 1960; [profile](https://en.gmw.cn/2022-08/05/content_37750837.htm)) — **1988:** member of the triple Everest traverse (PRC–Japan–Nepal), record time on the summit (~99 min); from 1993 — captain of the Tibetan team for all 14 eight-thousanders. With Penpa Tashi and Lodroe the team finishes the programme **12 July 2007** on Gasherbrum I **[confirmed]** — a collective Tibetan answer to the individual Western European “14×8000.”

## 2006–2010: while Berthod was silent — and the Torre Traverse

**[Sonnie Trotter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonnie_Trotter)** (Canada, b. 1979) — one of the most methodical trad climbers of his generation: **2006** — first free ascent of Cobra Crack, which Berthod had not managed to close, but for years returned to routes again if not fully sure he had climbed them absolutely honestly.

**Lee Cossey** (Australia, Blue Mountains; [profile/interview](https://www.powercompanyclimbing.com/blog/2019/8/7/ep-139-damage-control-in-chaos-with-lee-cossey)) — one of the country’s strongest all-round climbers (sport, bouldering, big walls), almost unknown outside a narrow circle. Freed lines on El Cap; **2009:** with Jake Bresnehan climbed (not first, but among the first after the 1991 first-ascent team) Riders on the Storm in Patagonia — a fully free ascent would come only in 2024 to a team including Sean Villanueva, Nico Favresse, Siebe Vanhee and Drew Smith. Australian *rock* beside the high-altitude canon of Macartney-Snape/Child — another axis of the same country.

**[Iker Pou](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iker_Pou) and [Eneko Pou](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eneko_Pou)** (Spain, Basque) — **13 August 2006:** Lurgorri (250 m, 6 pitches, Naranjo de Bulnes) — the first multipitch in history redpointed at 8c+. **2009:** Orbayu (510 m, 13 pitches, same) — then only the world’s fourth big wall redpointed at 8c.

**[Kei Taniguchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kei_Taniguchi_(mountaineer)) (1972–2015) and [Kazuya Hiraide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazuya_Hiraide) (1975–2024)** (Japan) — started with Everest litter clean-ups in 2002–2003 and grew into one of the world’s strongest partnerships, with first ascents on Spantik, Muztagh Ata and Shivling. In 2008 they opened “Samurai Direct” on the south-east face of Kamet (7756 m, India) in alpine style; for that in **2009** both received the Piolet d'Or, Taniguchi — the first woman. In 2013 the pair retreated under a serac on Shispare at 5700 m; **21 December 2015** Taniguchi died in a fall on Kurodake (Hokkaido), unclipping from the rope at a rest. Hiraide for a time doubted whether to continue climbing, but found a new permanent partner — **[Kenro Nakajima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenro_Nakajima)** — and with him received three more Piolets d'Or (2017/18, 2019/20, 2023/24), entering the trio of people in history with four or more awards. In **July 2024** Hiraide and Nakajima disappeared on the west face of K2.

**[Valeri Babanov](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valeri_Babanov)** ([site](https://www.babanov.com/), Russia; *mountaineering (technical / high-altitude)*) — **Piolet d'Or 2002** for a solo of the north face of Meru Central; **Piolet d'Or 2003/04** for the south pillar of East Nuptse with Yuri Koshelenko. Later — permanent jury member of the award.

**[Marko Prezelj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marko_Prezelj)** (Slovenia) — multiple Piolet d'Or winner; publicly criticizes the transformation of the award and ascents into a “résumé race” — beside record Himalayanism he holds the ethic of “why,” not “how many.”

**[Rolando Garibotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolando_Garibotti)** (Italy/Argentina) and **[Colin Haley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Haley)** (USA) — **January 2008:** first complete Torre Traverse (Standhardt–Herron–Egger–Cerro Torre) in alpine style **[confirmed]**. The traverse idea had for decades belonged to Ermanno Salvaterra and Italian teams; Garibotti — key historian and ethicist of Patagonia (incl. criticism of Maestri’s claim), raised in Bariloche: a voice of the local canon.

**Yan Dongdong** (China, 1984–2012; [Alpinist](https://alpinist.com/newswire/yan-dongdong/)) and **Zhou Peng** — **November 2009:** *The Free Spirits* on the south face of Siguniang (6250 m, Sichuan) — the first Chinese new line in alpine style on Siguniang **[confirmed]** (Golden Rhino / Chinese PdO analogue for Yan — three times). With **Li Lan** — first ascent of Wuse Shan (2010). Yan died **9 July 2012** in a Tien Shan crevasse. The Chinese canon after state expeditions gains a technical, “free” voice.


**[Scott Noy](https://scottnoy.wordpress.com/rocklandsbouldering/)** (South Africa) — local climber and guidebook author; from 2010 systematically documents Rocklands bouldering (editions 1.0 / 2x / 3.0). Through him Africa enters the canon also as infrastructure of the world bouldering scene — beside high-altitude first ascentionists like Vilane.

## 2011–2015: solitude, flute, sailboats, Kyrgyzstan hostages — and the fourth woman without oxygen

**[Sean Villanueva O’Driscoll](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Villanueva_O%E2%80%99Driscoll)** with brothers Nicolas and Olivier Favresse and Ben Ditto — Piolet d'Or for the **2010** Greenland expedition by sailboat; signature style — free climbing the hardest big walls with music (tin whistle, accordion) right on the wall, without military-expedition pathos.

**[Silvia Vidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvia_Vidal)** (Catalonia) — a multi-year series of solo first ascents in full autonomy: without radios, satellite links or weather forecasts. From the Karakoram (**Sol Solet** on Amin Brakk, 1999; later lines in Pakistan and on Baffin) to Patagonia.
- **2012:** Espiadimonis on Serranía Avelancha (Patagonia) — 32 days alone on the wall.
Philosophy of full autonomy (“no one outside manages my risk”) — one of the most consistent ethical gestures in big-wall climbing.

**[Jain Kim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain_Kim)** (South Korea) — at 153 cm built a competition style wholly contrary to power dynamics — smooth, static, based on balance; 2014 world champion, 14-time Asian champion.

**[Ashima Shiraishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashima_Shiraishi)** (USA, b. 2001) — New York prodigy (Rat Rock in Central Park): in **2016**, aged 14 — first woman on a **V15/8C** boulder (Horizon, Japan) **[confirmed]**; before that — junior age records V10–V14. Changes the idea of who and from where can set the world ceiling.

**[Alex Puccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Puccio)** (USA) — dominant figure of women’s competition bouldering in the 2010s (multiple US Nationals / World Cup podiums); outdoor V14+; face of “true” women’s bouldering beside comps.

**[Angela Eiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Eiter)** (Austria) — multiple world champion in lead; **October 2017:** first woman on **9b** (La Planta de Shiva, Villanueva del Rosario) **[confirmed]** — a separate milestone of women’s sport climbing.


**[Tommy Caldwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Caldwell)** (USA) by the time of this period’s main achievement had already lived through his own dark chapter: in August 2000, on the Kara-Su cliffs in Kyrgyzstan, he with **[Beth Rodden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beth_Rodden)**, Jason Smith and John Dickey was taken hostage by fighters of the Islamic Movement of Uzbekistan; the captives were used as human shields, and before their eyes a fifth captive — a Kyrgyz soldier — was shot. On the sixth day, when only one guard remained, Caldwell pushed him off a cliff to give his companions a chance to escape (the guard survived), after which all four walked out to people through the mountains. Caldwell returned to climbing and with **[Alex Honnold](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A5%D0%BE%D0%BD%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%B4,_%D0%90%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%BA%D1%81)** — **2015:** climbed Dawn Wall on El Capitan free in 19 days **[confirmed]**, and in **2014** they were first to complete the full traverse of the Fitz Roy massif (3405 m) from north to south.

**[Ueli Steck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ueli_Steck)**, “the Swiss Machine” (Switzerland, 1976–2017) — speed record holder on the north faces of the Alps. **October 2013:** claimed a solo ascent of Annapurna’s south face (8091 m) in 28 hours, by a new route, without oxygen — Piolet d'Or 2014. The claim is **[disputed]**: Steck lost his camera and had no GPS track, though two Sherpas saw his headlamp high on the wall. In April of the same 2013 he was at the centre of a conflict with Sherpas on Everest — a fight that began when his rope team accidentally knocked ice onto Sherpas working below; Simone Moro was climbing with him. Died 30 April 2017 on Nuptse, falling during an acclimatization climb.

**[Gerlinde Kaltenbrunner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerlinde_Kaltenbrunner)** (Austria) — **2011:** completed all 14 eight-thousanders on K2, the first woman fully without oxygen **[confirmed]**. She, Pasaban, Meroi and later Harila remain the only four women to close the full list — each in her own, fundamentally different style.

**[David Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lama) (1990–2019)** (Austria) — former world climbing champion, taught from age six by Peter Habeler. **2012:** rehabilitation of Maestri’s wall (see above). In 2015 he rescued Conrad Anker, who had a heart attack at altitude; in **2018** soloed the first ascent of Lunag Ri — after a failed attempt a year earlier with Anker. Died 16 April 2019 on Howse Peak (Canada) with **[Hansjörg Auer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hansj%C3%B6rg_Auer)** and Jess Roskelley.

**[Edurne Pasaban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edurne_Pasaban)** (Spain, Basque) — **17 May 2010:** first woman to complete all 14 eight-thousanders (with oxygen on some ascents) **[confirmed]**.

**[Oh Eun-sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oh_Eun-sun)** (South Korea) — claimed completion of 14×8000 before Pasaban (Annapurna, April 2010); the Kangchenjunga ascent (2009) is **[disputed]** / by the verdict of the Korean Alpine Federation and several Western chroniclers — **[not accepted by the community]** as a full summit. Oh herself later admitted she stopped below the top. The episode matters as a Korean knot of proof ethics beside Pasaban/Kaltenbrunner/Meroi.

**[Go Mi-young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_Mi-young)** (South Korea, 1967–2009) — strong high-altitude climber; died descending Nanga Parbat in 2009. The Korean women’s high-altitude line — not only the dispute over Oh Eun-sun’s record.


**[Marc-André Leclerc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc-Andre_Leclerc)** (Canada, 1992–2018) — soloing in Paterson and Patagonia as ethic, not content; died in March 2018 after The Corkscrew (Mendenhall Towers, Alaska). The documentary *The Alpinist* (2021) made him a posthumous icon.

**[Ali Sadpara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Sadpara)** (Pakistan, 1976–2021) — key figure of Pakistani high-altitude mountaineering; member of winter K2 attempts with Moro/Urubko; died on K2 in February 2021 with his son and a Spanish climber. Local hero of Pakistani high-altitude mountaineering.

**[Samina Baig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samina_Baig)** (Pakistan, b. 1990) — **19 May 2013:** first Pakistani woman on Everest **[confirmed]**; then Seven Summits. The women’s line of the Pakistani high-altitude row beside Aman, Sabir and Sadpara.

**[Kim Chang-ho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Chang-ho_(climber))** (South Korea, 1970–2018) — **2013:** completed 14×8000 in 7 years 10 months 6 days — then the world speed record **[confirmed]** (later surpassed by Purja’s oxygen push of 2019). Participant in the review of Oh Eun-sun’s Kangchenjunga claim. Died in October 2018 on Gurla Mandhata with his team. The “true” pole of the Korean school.

**[Arunima Sinha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunima_Sinha)** (India) — **21 May 2013:** first woman amputee on Everest **[confirmed]** (prosthesis; trained with Bachendri Pal’s support). Later — Seven Summits on a prosthesis. The Indian line after Pal/Yadav — not only “first woman,” but access to the summit after injury.


**[Kami Rita Sherpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kami_Rita_Sherpa)** (Nepal) — in these years begins the series that will make him absolute record holder for Everest ascents; commercial and labouring Sherpa pole inside the canon.

**[Cholita Climbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholita_Climbers)** / **[Cecilia Llusco Alaña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecilia_Llusco_Ala%C3%B1a)** (Bolivia, Aymara; Llusco b. 1985) — **December 2015:** a group of women in traditional polleras climbs Huayna Potosí (6088 m); later — Aconcagua (**23 January 2019**, first Aymara women on the summit) **[confirmed]**, Sajama and others. Lidia Huayllas, Dora Magueño, Ana Lia González, Elena Quispe and others — a Bolivian women’s voice in the Andean canon: guides and cooks of high-altitude expeditions become heroes of their own summits.

## 2016–2020: Olympus, women’s ceiling and the wingsuit hall

**[Adam Ondra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Ondra)** (Czechia; *sport climbing / comps / bouldering*) — **2010:** first to repeat Golpe de Estado (only the world’s second 9b) — his first route of that grade. Multiple world champion in comps (lead/boulder); **September 2017:** Silence — first confirmed 9c **[confirmed]**. Links competition and outdoor streams as few before him.

**[Alex Honnold](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A5%D0%BE%D0%BD%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%B4,_%D0%90%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%BA%D1%81)** (USA; *free solo / big walls*) — before Freerider already Half Dome solo and partnership with Caldwell (Nose speed, Dawn Wall); **3 June 2017:** free solo of Freerider on El Capitan **[confirmed]**; Academy Award for *Free Solo* in 2019 — the point after which outdoor ethics became mass cinema.

**[Magnus Midtbø](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9C%D0%B8%D0%B4%D1%82%D0%B1%D1%91,_%D0%9C%D0%B0%D0%B3%D0%BD%D1%83%D1%81)** (Norway) — strong competition and outdoor climber; ends his comps career around **2017** and moves to YouTube, becoming one of the main media conduits of the discipline for a mass audience.

**[Nirmal “Nims” Purja](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9F%D1%83%D1%80%D0%B4%D0%B6%D0%B0,_%D0%9D%D0%B8%D1%80%D0%BC%D0%B0%D0%BB)** (Nepal/United Kingdom, 1983–2026) — former Gurkha and SBS soldier. **2019:** all 14 eight-thousanders with oxygen in 6 months 6 days (“14 Peaks,” Netflix) **[confirmed]** — until 2023 the world speed record. Hyper-commercial pole of the canon: logistics, oxygen, media, brand.

**[Janja Garnbret](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%93%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%BD%D0%B1%D1%80%D0%B5%D1%82,_%D0%AF%D0%BD%D1%8C%D1%8F)** (Slovenia; *comps / bouldering / sport climbing*) — dominated so thoroughly that the question at meets was more often not “who will win” but “by how much”; **2019:** all six stages of the Bouldering World Cup in a season; Olympic gold Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024.

**[Shauna Coxsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shauna_Coxsey)** (United Kingdom) — overall IFSC Bouldering World Cup winner **2016 and 2017**; face of British women’s comps before the Olympics; after Tokyo left competitions for outdoor.

**[Aleksandra Mirosław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandra_Miros%C5%82aw)** (Poland; *comps (speed)*) — absolute dominant of **speed** climbing: Olympic gold Tokyo and Paris, world speed records. Without her the Olympic canon of the 2020s looks as if speed were a “third discipline for show.”

**[Deng Lijuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Lijuan)** (China, b. 2000) — silver Paris 2024 in speed (final with Mirosław, 6.18 s — personal best) **[confirmed]**; **Wu Peng** — silver in men’s speed there. **[Pan Yufei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Yufei)** and **[Song Yiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_Yiling)** — Chinese comps debut at Tokyo 2020 (combined format). The Chinese canon after eight-thousanders and Sichuan walls gains an Olympic comps voice.

**[Akiyo Noguchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akiyo_Noguchi)** (Japan, b. 1989) — four-time overall Bouldering World Cup winner; bronze Tokyo 2020 (combined) **[confirmed]** — the first Olympic generation of Japanese comps, retiring at the peak of a home Olympics. From **2021** married to **[Tomoa Narasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomoa_Narasaki)** — multiple world/World Cup boulder champion, 4th at Tokyo 2020; a Japanese comps dynasty under one roof.

**[Sorato Anraku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorato_Anraku)** (Japan, b. 2006) — in debut season **2023** first to win overall both Boulder and Lead World Cup **[confirmed]**; silver Paris 2024 (boulder+lead). **[Ai Mori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ai_Mori)** — world lead champion (**2023**), first Japanese woman with a WC title in difficulty. The Japanese canon after Tabei/Uemura/Miura gains a world-class comps machine beside the Korean and Chinese.


**[Margo Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margo_Hayes)** (USA; *sport climbing*) — **2017:** first woman on a consensus **9a+** (La Rambla, then Papichulo and others) **[confirmed]** — a frontier after Bereziartu/Eiter on the way to Brooke Raboutou’s 9b+.


**[Andrzej Bargiel](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%91%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B3%D0%B5%D0%BB%D1%8C,_%D0%90%D0%BD%D0%B4%D0%B6%D0%B5%D0%B9)** (Poland) — **22 July 2018:** first complete ski descent from the summit of K2 to base camp, without oxygen on the ascent **[confirmed]**.

**[William Bosi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Bosi)** (Scotland, b. 1998; *bouldering / sport climbing*) — started climbing at 6; by 2025 became the first person in history to climb five 9A/V17 boulders (Alphane 2022, Burden of Dreams 2023, Return of the Sleepwalker and Spots of Time 2024, Realm of Tor'ment 2025; the last a *proposed* 9A, awaiting repeats), preparing for some on 3D-printed copies of real holds; in 2025 also became the first Briton to climb 9b+ (Excalibur, second ascent after Stefano Ghisolfi).

**[Nalle Hukkataival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalle_Hukkataival)** (Finland; *bouldering*) — first ascent of Burden of Dreams (first consensus 9A/V17); without the authors of the lines “Bosi’s five 9As” are unreadable.

**[Shawn Raboutou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawn_Raboutou)** / **[Aidan Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aidan_Roberts)** — authors of Alphane and Spots of Time; the generation setting the bouldering ceiling beside Bosi.

**[Nives Meroi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nives_Meroi)** (Italy) — with husband Romano Benet spent years aiming to become the first couple to climb all 14 eight-thousanders together, without oxygen and without high-altitude porter help. In 2008, already at Camp 4 on Kangchenjunga, Romano fell seriously ill; Nives brought him down, refusing an almost guaranteed record. The next two years went to his treatment. Both finished the programme together in **2017** **[confirmed]**.

**[Alexander Odintsov](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9E%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%BD%D1%86%D0%BE%D0%B2,_%D0%90%D0%BB%D0%B5%D0%BA%D1%81%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B4%D1%80_%D0%AE%D1%80%D1%8C%D0%B5%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%87)** (Russia; *big walls / competition alpinism*) — Russian big walls (Ak-Su, Trango and others); the “Russian Path — Walls of the World” project; Piolet d'Or Carrière 2020.

**[Alexander Ruchkin](http://www.alpklubspb.ru/persona/ruchkin.htm)** (Russia, 1963–2015; *big walls / technical mountaineering*) — one of the strongest wall climbers of his generation: Ak-Su, Jannu (north face, Piolet d'Or with Odintsov’s team) and dozens of 6A–6B routes. Died **27 August 2015** with Vyacheslav Ivanov on the south face of Huandoy Sur (Cordillera Blanca) **[confirmed]**.

**[Yevgeny Vinogradsky](http://russianclimb.com/russian/everest_stat.html)** (Russia; *high-altitude mountaineering*) — one of the most productive Russian high-altitude climbers on Everest (four full ascents and an almost-fifth); a bridge between the Soviet Himalayan school of the 1980s–90s and the commercial era of the 2000s.

**Wingsuit / BASE as continuation of the wall** (a subchapter of the canon, not a footnote):
- **[Ueli Gegenschatz](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ueli_Gegenschatz)** (Switzerland, 1971–2009) — early wingsuit BASE from alpine walls; died jumping in Zurich.
- **Soul Flyers** — **Vince Reffet** (†2020) and **Fred Fugen** (France): flights from alpine and urban objects, the bridge “mountaineering ↔ flight.”
- **[Gary Connery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Connery)** (United Kingdom) — **23 May 2012:** first documented wingsuit jump with landing **without parachute deployment** (onto cardboard boxes) **[confirmed]** — a media, but technically important, frontier of controlled flight.
- **[Jokke Sommer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jokke_Sommer)** (Norway) and **Espen Fadnes** ([espenfadnes.com](https://espenfadnes.com/)) — the *Perfect Flight* generation: proximity flying in the Alps (incl. under the Aiguille du Midi bridge), video as a way to make a niche sport visible without turning the mountain into a “content siege.”
- Beside them in the canon remain **Valery Rozov** and **Dean Potter** (see above): altitude as a launching pad, not only the goal of ascent.

## 2021–2026: records, K2 tragedy, Broad Peak and returns

**[Sean Villanueva O’Driscoll](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Villanueva_O%E2%80%99Driscoll)** — **5–10 February 2021:** first solo traverse of the Fitz Roy massif from south to north (“Moonwalk Traverse”) **[confirmed]**; Piolet d'Or in November 2022. **February 2024:** with Nico Favresse, Siebe Vanhee and Drew Smith — first complete free (team-free) ascent of Riders on the Storm.

**[Nirmal “Nims” Purja](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9F%D1%83%D1%80%D0%B4%D0%B6%D0%B0,_%D0%9D%D0%B8%D1%80%D0%BC%D0%B0%D0%BB)** — **2021:** with nine Nepalis — first winter ascent of K2 **[confirmed]**; later — speed record for 14×8000 without oxygen (2 years 150 days / ~2 years 5 months, finish on Shishapangma **4 October 2024**). Died **30 July 2026**: an avalanche hit his expedition on Broad Peak between Camps II and III, killing all ten in the group **[confirmed]** (Elite Exped / Alpine Club of Pakistan / BBC).

**[Mingma Gyalje Sherpa (“Mingma G”)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mingma_Gyalje_Sherpa)** (Nepal) — member of winter K2 2021; **4 October 2024** on Shishapangma completes 14×8000 **without oxygen** — first Nepali in that style **[confirmed]** (the same day Purja, a British citizen of Nepali origin, also finishes; both climbed together). Guide for Imagine Nepal; labouring Sherpa pole closing the oxygenless list, not only the commercial repetition record.

**[Kristin Harila](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A5%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B8%D0%BB%D0%B0,_%D0%9A%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B8%D0%BD)** (Norway) and **Tenjen Sherpa** — **27 July 2023:** speed record for 14×8000 (92 days), beating Purja’s oxygen record **[confirmed]**. The same day Pakistani porter **Muhammad Hassan** died: a drone filmed several teams, including Harila’s group, passing him. Harila states her team spent more than an hour and a half trying to stabilize him. A Pakistani investigation found no personal guilt of Harila’s; there is no community consensus **[disputed]** ethical assessment of the episode.

**[Didier Berthod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Berthod)** — **14 May 2024**, after 19 years and 13 years of monastic life, at 43 finally free-climbed Cobra Crack **[confirmed]**.

**Valeri Babanov** — in 2021 sat on the Piolets d'Or jury as a former award winner.

**[Ines Papert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ines_Papert)** and **[Mayan Smith-Gobat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayan_Smith-Gobat)** — free variation toward Riders on the Storm (2016), without which the free ascent of 2024 would have been different; women’s big-wall contour of Patagonia.

**[Brette Harrington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brette_Harrington)** (USA/Canada) — free solo of big walls; “true” women’s big wall outside the Olympic circuit.

**[Brooke Raboutou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooke_Raboutou)** (USA; *comps / sport climbing*) — Olympic silver Paris 2024; **April 2025:** first woman on **9b+** (Excalibur) **[confirmed]** — sister of Shawn Raboutou, daughter of champions Didier Raboutou / Robyn Erbesfield: a dynasty, not an accident.

**[Laura Rogora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Rogora)** (Italy) — first Italian woman on 9b; comps + outdoor at elite level; European women’s sport ceiling beside Garnbret and Raboutou.

**Felipe Camargo** (Brazil, b. 1991; [TNF bio](https://www.thenorthface.com/en-us/about-us/athletes/felipe-camargo)) — first Brazilian on **5.15b/9b** and **V15**; **2023–2024:** first ascents of Auto Retrato (5.15a) and Abaporu (5.15b) in Brazil — first lines of those grades in South America **[confirmed]**. Latin American sport climbing leaves the role of “Europe’s audience.”

**Eliseu Frechou** (Brazil; [profile](https://geartips.club/blog/en/between-mountains-and-stories-eliseu-frechou-helped-build-climbing-in-brazil/)) — from the late 1980s builds the infrastructure of Pedra do Baú / São Bento do Sapucaí: first Brazilian climbing school Montanhismus, hundreds of first ascents, newspaper *Mountain Voices*, film *Terra de Gigantes*. Without him the Brazilian outdoor canon (and later Camargo) has no local soil.

**Latin American comps** — Curitiba hosts the **first IFSC World Cup in South America** (**May 2025**) **[confirmed]**: the continent ceases to be only a supplier of outdoor talent and becomes a node of the competition calendar.

**[Babsi Zangerl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babsi_Zangerl)** (Austria) and **[Jacopo Larcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacopo_Larcher)** (Italy) — free climbing of big walls and trad at the limit (El Cap, Alps, Patagonia); “true” big-wall contour of the 2010s–2020s beside Caldwell and Villanueva.

**[Nina Caprez](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Caprez)** (Switzerland, b. 1986) — from comps into the hardest multipitches: Silbergeier (8b+, first woman, 2011), Neverending Story, Delicatessen, Yeah Man (first women’s free, 2023). European women’s big wall/multipitch beside Zangerl — not only the sport ceiling of 9b+, but long walls.

**Patagonia in the 2020s** continues the Garibotti/Haley/Villanueva line: free ascents of old sieges of the 1980s remain the ethical horizon — *cleaner*, not necessarily faster.

## Matrix of philosophies

| Type | Philosophy | Representatives | Contribution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ascetic pioneer** | Full autonomy, climbing as an end in itself | Winkler, Arnold, Vidal, Leclerc, Yamanoi, Kapadia | Set a model independent of media |
| **Clean-style ideologue** | Refusal of “dishonest” means | Preuss, Robbins, Hill, Bachar, Prezelj | Formed climbing ethics |
| **Gear and technique revolutionary** | Changed the physical possibility of going further | Chouinard, Jardine, Albert, Güllich, Allain, Herzog, Nicole | Without them modern grades would be unreachable |
| **Small team instead of siege** | Team minimalism instead of the military expedition model | Messner, Habeler, Kurtyka, Loretan, Buhl (1957), House | 4 people without porters stronger than an army |
| **Rock-and-roll tricksters** | Serious ascents without pathos | Villanueva, Favresse brothers, Ditto | Free climbing of big walls without military discipline |
| **Professional athlete** | Integration into sport, records within ethics | Ondra, Garnbret, Taniguchi, Hiraide, Boukreev, Bosi, Jain Kim, Noguchi, Narasaki, Anraku, Mori | Raised the ceiling of the discipline from inside the community |
| **Media educator** | Popularization through personal brand | Midtbø, Honnold (partly), Edlinger, Sharma | Brought a mass audience into climbing |
| **Hyper-merchant / labouring high-altitude climber** | Record as product *or* the mountain as profession | Purja, Harila, Kami Rita, Apa Sherpa, Todd | Changed the mechanics of high-altitude mountaineering — and exposed the costs; made Sherpa labour visible |
| **Pioneer against the norm** | Achievement as a challenge to expectations of gender, race, era | Tabei, Hargreaves, Rutkiewicz, Meroi, Kaltenbrunner, Pasaban, Pasang Lhamu, Lhakpa, Vilane, Shatayeva, Cholitas, Bachendri Pal, Santosh Yadav, Bradey, Arunima Sinha | Showed that barriers were social |
| **Exposed mystifier / disputed claim** | Claimed achievement under question | Maestri **[not accepted]**, Česen **[disputed]**, Steck/Annapurna **[disputed]**, Rouhling/Akira grade **[disputed]** | “A climber’s word” cannot always be verified |
| **Convicted criminal who became a pioneer** | Criminal biography outside the mountains, followed by a legitimate contribution | Henry Todd | Past and contribution to the discipline can be separated |
| **Rescuers at the cost of their own ascent** | Refusal of the goal for another person’s life | Moro, Urubko, Lama, Meroi, Boukreev | A value hierarchy that sport rarely records in records |
| **Extreme sport as continuation of mountaineering** | The mountain as a starting point for flight | Rozov, Potter, Gegenschatz, Soul Flyers, Connery, Sommer/Fadnes | Showed that the vertical world does not end on the summit |
| **Subject of the colonial periphery** | Local guide/climber as author of history | Tenzing, Gombu, Mehdi, Aman, Sabir, Sadpara, Samina Baig, Pasang Lhamu, Ang Rita, Babu Chiri, Kami Rita, Mingma G, Gongbu | Without them the canon remains a European club |
| **Patagonian / Andean canon** | Place as a school of style, not a range for visiting stars | Terray/Magnone, Morales Arnao, Marco Cruz, Mesili/Sánchez, Ruiz, Delgado, Viñals, Cholitas, Karo, Garibotti/Haley, Villanueva, Carsolio/Potrero, Frechou, Camargo | From the Cordillera Blanca and Santa Marta to Central America and Brazil |
| **State Asian project** | The mountain as national mobilization | Wang Fuzhou / Qu / Gongbu, Pan Duo, Ciren Duoji / Tibetan team of 14, Li Zhixin/Wang Yongfeng, Krylenko/Abalakovs, Korean school (Um, Park, Kim) | Parallel USSR–PRC–Korea: the individual inside the machine |
| **Chinese technical mountaineering** | Alpine style on home walls after state expeditions | Yan Dongdong / Zhou Peng, Li Lan | Siguniang and Sichuan as China’s “free” answer |
| **Olympic speed** | The vertical as pure power and reaction | Mirosław, Deng Lijuan, Wu Peng, (context of Garnbret/Raboutou in boulder+lead) | Third Olympic discipline with its own canon of heroes |
| **Women’s difficulty ceiling** | Closing the grade gap | Bereziartu, Eiter, Hayes, Shiraishi, Coxsey, Rogora, Raboutou, Garnbret | From the first women’s 9a to 9b+ and Olympic dominance |

## Comparative table by eras

| Era | Dominant philosophy | Key ethical fork | Signature figures |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1900–1930s | Romanticism of the solo, the “piton war” | Mechanical protection versus personal mastery | Whymper, Winkler, Preuss, Dibona |
| 1914–1920 | Mountains as theatre of war | Military necessity versus human life | White War in the Dolomites |
| 1930–1945 | Mountains in the service of the state | Personal success versus propaganda and repression | Comici, Harrer, Cassin, Abalakovs, Krylenko, Wiessner |
| 1950–1970s | State mountaineering of the USSR vs. Western individualism | Glory of the first ascentionist versus a partner’s life | Hillary/Tenzing, Bonatti/Mehdi, Buhl, Khergiani, Shatayeva, Tabei |
| 1966–1980s | Gear revolution and the small team | Technical progress versus preservation of the mountain | Chouinard, Jardine, Kurtyka, Zawada/Wielicki, Messner/Habeler, Aman |
| 1980–2000s | Dirtbags, winter Himalayanism, cancelled and exposed records | Community recognition versus the reality of the achievement | Güllich, Kukuczka, Česen, Maestri, Pasang Lhamu, Nicole, Macartney-Snape, Bradey, Hall/1996 |
| 2010–2026 | Digital era, record as media product, visible Sherpa labour, women’s 9b+ ceiling | Speed and record versus basic humanity | Villanueva, Steck, Purja, Harila, Kami Rita, Leclerc, Bosi, Garnbret/Raboutou, Garibotti |
